TTS Testing Issue
Text-to-Speech Affecting Test Validity

Until now, Kindergarten and Grade 1 Reading tests included TTS on all elements, creating two problems:

  • False positives: Students pass tests using listening skills without actual reading ability
  • False negatives: Students with reading skills fail when answering from memory rather than checking text

Resolution: TTS is being removed from all elements except question prompts on reading tests.

Understanding the Alignment

ORF Grade = the fluency level a student is WORKING ON (ORF=2 means mastered G1 fluency, working on G2)

HMG = the comprehension grade a student has MASTERED (HMG=1 means mastered G1, working on G2)

Expected alignment: ORF = HMG + 1 (fluency working level matches comprehension working level)

Fluency-Behind Students (56%): Targeted Intervention is Working

The majority of students haven't passed the fluency benchmark for their current working grade. These students are now kept at their ORF level with targeted comprehension practice. Results are strong:

  • Highest actual growth: 4.68x (131% of prediction)
  • Lowest severe underperformance: Only 14.8%
  • The intervention policy change from "promote anyway" is paying off
Concern: ALIGNED Students Have HIGHEST Severe Underperformance (32.3%)

Surprisingly, students with ORF = HMG+1 (fluency matches comprehension level) show the highest severe underperformance rate. This group may be falling through the cracks:

  • No intervention triggers: They don't flag for fluency support OR comprehension holdback
  • Possibly coasting: Meeting minimum thresholds without deep mastery
  • Less momentum: Fluency-behind students benefit from targeted intervention focus

Investigation needed: What differentiates aligned students who succeed vs. those who severely underperform?

Fluency-Ahead Students (2%): Small Sample, Best Outcomes

Only 3 students have fluency ahead of HMG+1. These students are held back until they master comprehension tests. With 66.7% meeting predictions and 0% severe underperformance, this policy appears appropriate, though sample size is too small for conclusions.

Pattern 5: Prediction Model Validity Analysis

Key Questions This Analysis Answers

Q: Was the Engineering prediction model accurate?

No. Only ~36% of students met Engineering predictions vs 87% for NWEA projections. The model systematically over-predicts because:

  • Engineering defaults to 4.0x NWEA for most students
  • Actual median growth is only 2.75x NWEA
  • NWEA bar is 1.0x (industry standard), which 87% exceed
Q: Should ORF Grade be incorporated into predictions?

Yes, but for risk flagging, not prediction adjustment. Both ORF groups have similar met-prediction rates (~35-37%), but ALIGNED students have nearly DOUBLE the severe underperformance rate (34% vs 19%). ORF identifies WHO needs extra monitoring.

Q: Is the 4.0x default the core issue?

Yes. The 4.0x default is too high for ALL students - median actual is ~2.5-2.8x regardless of ORF alignment. The difference between groups is not in average performance but in variance and risk:

  • Fluency-behind: Higher variance - some outliers with 10x-29x growth inflate the mean, but most still miss predictions
  • Aligned: Highest severe underperformance (34%) - these students need monitoring
Critical Finding: Engineering Predictions Are Systematically Too High

Comparing the Engineering team predictions vs NWEA industry-standard projections reveals a significant calibration issue.

Key Insight: Both Groups Miss Predictions Equally, But ALIGNED Students Have Double the Severe Miss Rate

Both fluency-behind and aligned students have similar met-prediction rates (~35-37%). The critical difference: 34.4% of aligned students severely underperform vs only 19.4% of fluency-behind students. Aligned students don't trigger intervention flags but have the highest risk of catastrophic underperformance.

Why Do Fluency-Behind Students Have Higher Mean Growth?

The mean growth for fluency-behind students (4.62x) appears much higher than aligned students (3.67x), but this is misleading:

  • Median growth is similar: 2.82x (behind) vs 2.42x (aligned)
  • 7 fluency-behind students with extreme outlier growth (10x-29x) inflate the mean
  • These outliers may be data anomalies or students with unusual circumstances
  • The typical student in both groups achieves ~2.5-2.8x NWEA, well below the 4.0x target
Recommendation: Incorporate ORF into the Prediction Model for Risk Flagging

ORF alignment should be used primarily for identifying at-risk students, not for adjusting prediction targets. Both groups have similar median growth (~2.5-2.8x), so the prediction itself doesn't need to differ. What differs is the risk profile:

Additional Finding: XP Achievement

Only 1 of 107 students (0.9%) earned expected XP. 100% of very-wrong predictions were for students who did NOT meet XP expectations. This suggests either:

  • XP expectations are unrealistically high, OR
  • Predictions assume XP completion that rarely occurs

Bottom Line: What Should Change in the Prediction Model?

  1. Recalibrate the 4.0x default to ~2.75x. The blanket 4.0x expectation is unrealistic (only ~36% meet it). Median actual growth is 2.75x across all groups. Setting a more realistic target would bring success rates closer to NWEA's 87%.
  2. Add ORF as a risk flag, not a prediction modifier. ORF alignment doesn't change predicted growth (both groups have ~2.5-2.8x median), but ALIGNED students (ORF = HMG+1) should be flagged for additional monitoring due to their 34% severe underperformance rate.
  3. Investigate why ALIGNED students have the worst outcomes. These students appear to have "matching" fluency and comprehension but have double the catastrophic failure rate. Something is being missed in assessment.
  4. Account for summer learning loss. W-W predictions should factor in the ~3.9 RIT point summer loss rather than assuming continuous growth.
